Warm and understated, not loud festival stock. The feeling is the quiet
moment of handing something over that turns out to matter — a gift that
becomes a trip, not a voucher.

Show a real Diwali evening at home: someone lighting a row of diyas seen
from behind, hands passing a wrapped gift across a table, a family
gathered at the doorway shot from the courtyard so nobody faces camera.
Mithai boxes, marigold, worn brass, a slim card among the presents.
Warm candlelight from below, deep shadow at the edges.

People should be in it and doing something real — never posed, never
looking at the camera, never a close-up portrait or a staged smile.

Let the real Diwali colours carry it — amber, marigold orange, gold, deep
shadow. Do not wash the scene in blue.

No aircraft, airports or runways. Clutter-free, generous empty space in
the top third for text, top-right corner clear.

Tone for the line: cheeky and warm, a wink rather than a sentimental
Diwali greeting.
