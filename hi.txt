Maximizing Smart Narrative in Power BI
The Smart Narrative visual is more customizable than most people realize. Here's how to actually squeeze performance out of it:

Understanding What You're Working With
Smart Narrative has two modes:

Auto-generated — Power BI writes it, you have little control
Custom with dynamic values — You write the narrative, embed live DAX values inside it

The second mode is where the real power is, and most people never use it.

The Right Way to Set It Up
Step 1 — Switch to Custom Narrative Mode
Insert Smart Narrative → click inside it → "Add a value" button appears. This lets you embed dynamic DAX expressions inline inside your text.
Your narrative becomes:

"Sales this month reached [+Value: SUM(Sales)] which is [+Value: Growth%] compared to last period..."

Every bracketed value is live and filter-responsive.
Step 2 — Pre-build Your KPI Measures in DAX First
Don't embed raw aggregations in the narrative. Build clean measures:
daxRevenue Growth % = 
DIVIDE([Revenue] - [Revenue LY], [Revenue LY], 0)

Performance Flag = 
SWITCH(TRUE(),
    [Revenue Growth %] > 0.1, "strong growth",
    [Revenue Growth %] > 0, "moderate growth",
    [Revenue Growth %] < 0, "declining",
    "flat"
)

Top Region = 
CALCULATE(
    FIRSTNONBLANK(Region[Region Name], 1),
    TOPN(1, VALUES(Region[Region Name]), [Revenue])
)
Then your narrative reads naturally:

"Revenue shows [Performance Flag] at [Revenue] . Top region is [Top Region]."

Step 3 — Write the Narrative Like an Analyst Would
Structure it in 3 blocks:
HEADLINE
"[Month] performance is [Status]. Revenue hit [Revenue] — [Growth%] vs target."

DETAIL
"Best performing area: [Top Region] at [Region Revenue].
Biggest drag: [Bottom Region] at [Bottom Revenue]."

ALERT (conditional)
"⚠ [X] KPIs are below threshold this period."

Making It "Adapt" to Filters
The Smart Narrative already reacts to slicers — but only if your measures use proper filter context. The key rules:
Do this — measures that respect context:
daxRevenue = SUM(Sales[Amount])  -- responds to any filter
Avoid this — hardcoded ALL() that ignores filters:
daxRevenue = CALCULATE(SUM(Sales[Amount]), ALL(Date))  -- breaks narrative
Also sync your Smart Narrative visual with your slicers explicitly via View → Sync Slicers — otherwise it may not update on all filter changes.

Conditional Language (Pseudo-Intelligence)
You can make the narrative feel smart by using IF/SWITCH inside your value expressions:
daxSummary Sentence = 
"Revenue is " & FORMAT([Revenue], "#,##0") & 
" which is " & 
IF([Revenue Growth %] > 0, 
   "above target by " & FORMAT([Revenue Growth %], "0.0%"),
   "BELOW target by " & FORMAT(ABS([Revenue Growth %]), "0.0%") & " ⚠"
)
This makes the tone of the narrative change based on actual performance — it reads differently in good months vs bad months.

Alert card — only shows text when something is off-threshold (use a measure that returns blank when everything is fine)
