# Pipeline addendum — how this skill is applied inside the AI Video studio

This file is **not** part of the upstream `visual-skills` release. It is the house
layer that binds the skill to this backend. Read it last, after `dramaturgy.md`,
`universal-rules.md` and `veo.md`. Where it conflicts with those files it is
because the pipeline imposes a hard constraint — obey the constraint, keep the
craft.

---

## 1. The generator is Veo, and Veo is short

Every scene in this pipeline becomes exactly one Veo generation. Veo renders
**4, 6 or 8 seconds** and nothing else. A 90 second film is 12 generations that
ffmpeg accumulates into one file — it is never one long prompt.

Consequences that are not negotiable:

- **You do not choose the durations.** The segment plan is computed before you
  are called and handed to you. Write to the seconds you are given, scene by
  scene. Do not merge scenes, do not split them, do not add or drop one.
- **Every scene must be a complete shot in its own right.** It has to survive
  being generated in isolation by a model with no memory of the other scenes.
- **The last frame of scene N and the first frame of scene N+1 must match** in
  pose, light, colour temperature and lens height. That match is the only reason
  the cut works in the edit. Say the match out loud in `continuityNote`.

## 2. Budget the action to the seconds you were given

A 4 second shot holds one action. An 8 second shot holds two, at most three.
Cramming a five-beat sequence into 4 seconds produces the speed-up artefact.

| Seconds | Beats inside the shot | Dialogue |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 1 primary action, 1 reaction | up to ~8 words |
| 6 | 2 actions, or 1 action + held reaction | up to ~14 words |
| 8 | 2–3 actions with a pause | up to ~20 words |

Veo caps spoken audio at 8 seconds per clip. Read every line aloud at normal
pace before you keep it.

## 3. The continuity block is repeated in every single prompt

The model has no memory between generations. Each scene prompt restates, in
full: character identity, exact wardrobe, location, palette with named colours,
the lighting constant with its direction, and the dominant camera grammar.

Yes, in all twelve prompts. Yes, verbatim. That repetition is the only thing
holding the film together.

## 4. Aspect ratio is fixed for the whole film

Veo natively renders `16:9` and `9:16`. Square and 4:5 deliverables are
generated at `9:16` and centre-cropped by the renderer. Never write framing that
depends on information at the top or bottom of a 9:16 frame when the delivery
ratio is 1:1 or 4:5 — keep the subject in the middle third.

## 5. Native audio is on — but you do not write the `Audio:` line

Veo generates synchronised audio, and `veo.md` is right that it needs explicit
labels. This pipeline supplies two of them for you, so **override `veo.md` on
this one point**:

- **Never write an `Audio:` or a `Duration:` line inside `prompt`.** Both are
  appended in code — `Audio:` from the separate `audio` field you return, and
  `Duration:` from the segment plan. Any you write yourself are discarded before
  the prompt reaches Veo.
- **Put the sound bed in the `audio` field**: room tone, texture, body sound,
  the motif. No label, no duration, just the direction.
- **`SFX:` and `Says:` lines still belong in `prompt`** when there is a punctual
  event or a spoken line. Those are kept exactly as written.

A music bed is mixed in later by the renderer, so **do not ask Veo for score**.
If a scene should be quiet, the `audio` field simply says `room tone only`.

## 6. Per-scene hard requirements

A scene is rejected by the automated audit and sent back for a rewrite if any of
these is missing. Check them yourself before you emit.

1. **Environmental pressure** — a physical fact of the space carrying the
   emotion. Not "a nice room".
2. **Physical micro-action** — the emotion rendered on a body. Not "she is
   happy".
3. **Sound anchor or visual motif** — tied to the spine of the piece and
   recurring across scenes.
4. **Lens** — a stated focal length.
5. **One primary camera move**, with the reason it moves ("what changed?").
6. **Named light source and direction** — "cold fridge light as key from
   frame-right", not "beautiful lighting".
7. **Named colours** — "cold blue-grey shadows, desaturated skin", not
   "cinematic colours".
8. **An explicit final image** — the frame the shot ends on.
9. **The continuity block**, restated.

## 7. Banned vocabulary

These words never render. Each one is a placeholder for a detail that was not
invented. They fail the audit automatically:

`cinematic`, `cinematic masterpiece`, `professional`, `high quality`,
`highest quality`, `best quality`, `masterpiece`, `stunning`, `epic`, `amazing`,
`beautiful lighting`, `beautiful light`, `dynamic camera`, `intense moment`,
`powerful scene`, `breathtaking`, `award-winning`, `award winning`,
`highly detailed`, `8k`, `4k`, `hyperrealistic`, `ultra realistic`,
`ultra-realistic`, `photorealistic masterpiece`, `he is sad`, `she is angry`,
`he is afraid`.

The one exception: `cinematic naturalism` as a named look inside the style block
is allowed, because it denotes a specific grade rather than praise.

## 8. Marketing briefs are still drama

Most briefs into this studio are ads. That does not exempt them from the scene
formula. A product film still needs a desire, an obstacle, a crack and a final
image — "beautiful hero shot of product" is not a shot function. Use the
commercial product-drama pattern:

```text
lifestyle setup → product reveal → macro texture → human reaction → use moment → hero product shot
```

The brand's payoff belongs on the `impact` and `aftermath` beats, not smeared
across every scene.

## 9. What you return

Structured JSON only, matching the schema supplied in the request. No prose
around it, no markdown fences. Prompts themselves are always written in English
even when the brief is in another language, because Veo performs better in
English; the `summary`, `title` and `treatment` fields follow the user's
language.
