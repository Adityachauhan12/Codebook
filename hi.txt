"""IndiGo brand guardrails for the video pipeline.

Single source of truth for brand policy in this codebase. The image and text
policies live in the sibling marketing repo (``prompts/platform_*_prompt.py``);
this file carries the shared rules that survive the jump to motion, plus
``INDIGO_VIDEO_BRAND_POLICY`` — the video-specific document, which has no
counterpart there because video breaks brand in ways stills cannot.

Three things make video different, and they are why this file exists:

1. **Every clip is an independent generation.** Veo has no memory between the
   4/6/8-second segments the duration planner produces, so a palette stated
   once drifts off-brand by the middle of the film. Brand has to be restated in
   every single prompt, exactly like the continuity block.
2. **Generative video cannot render a wordmark.** Asking Veo for the IndiGo
   logo produces a misspelt, wrong-coloured mark every time. The brand book
   already forbids this by another route — "wordmark should always be used from
   the master file, and should not be typed" — so the mark is banned from the
   prompt and composited from the master asset at render time.
3. **Motion carries brand meaning that a still cannot.** "Effortless" is a
   camera behaviour before it is a colour.
"""

from __future__ import annotations

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Colour
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Pantone references only. There are deliberately no hex values here.
#
# Nothing in this pipeline paints a colour itself: the prompt layer steers Veo
# with colour *language* (a diffusion model responds to "deep indigo navy-blue",
# not to a number), and the brand mark is composited from the master artwork,
# which already carries its own colour.
#
# Hex is needed only if we later render colour ourselves — supers, an end card,
# letterbox bars, or a grade. Take those values from the brand book at that
# point. A guessed Pantone-to-sRGB conversion sitting in a file called policy.py
# will eventually be trusted by someone, and shipping a "close enough" blue is
# the exact failure this module exists to prevent.
#
# NOTE: the two source policy files disagreed on the light blue (291 C in the
# top-level rules, 284 C in the image policy). Confirmed as **Pantone 291 C**.

INDIGO_DARK_BLUE_PANTONE = "Pantone 2738 C"
INDIGO_LIGHT_BLUE_PANTONE = "Pantone 291 C"

#: How the palette should be *described* to a generative model. Colour names
#: steer a diffusion model; hex codes do not.
INDIGO_PALETTE_LANGUAGE = (
    "deep indigo navy-blue with a violet lean (Pantone 2738 C) as the dominant colour, "
    "pale sky blue (Pantone 291 C) as the secondary, and clean white as the breathing space"
)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Shared rules — the brand book, as it applies to any medium
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

INDIGO_BRAND_POLICY = """
HARD RULES:
- Brand name is always written as: IndiGo (capital I, capital G). Never Indigo, INDIGO,
  indigo, Air IndiGo, IndiGo Airways, IndiGo Airlines, or goindigo
- Never turn the name into a verb: no IndiGoing, IndiGoes, IndiGone, IndiWent
- URL is always written as goIndiGo.in (I and G upper case)
- Logo has 20 dots and must always appear in correct proportion and direction
- Wordmark always comes from the master file and is never typed or recreated
- Typeface: Bauhaus. Light weight for titles and headlines — never bold, semibold,
  medium, or regular
- Always sentence case. Never ALL CAPS. Title case only for names, properties, and assets
- Never say anything negative about IndiGo
- Always polite and professional
- Never place the logo on an aircraft tail; place it in a corner that suits the composition

SOFT RULES:
- Black, dark blue, or light blue background -> white logo
- Arrow-plane may be used creatively (patterns, circles) but must always imply
  forward/upward motion, never pure decoration
- Where a logo meets a building, start it from the building rather than overlapping it
- Primary colour: Dark Blue (Pantone 2738 C). Secondary: Light Blue (Pantone 291 C)
- Simple, eye-catching, non-controversial subject that adapts to many sizes
- Soft colours, balanced composition, simple design
- Integrate pop culture and modern trends where they genuinely fit
"""


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emotional register
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The craft layer's beat map is built for short drama: its own labels read
# "Hero already in tension. Problem on screen.", "Hero tries to hold control.",
# "A detail appears that breaks the hero's position.", "Breath tightens."
#
# Handed to a writer scene by scene, that produces an anxious person in an
# airport — which is both off-brand ("effortless, hassle-free") and
# reputationally wrong for an airline. The dramatic *shape* is still right; a
# brand film needs a turn or it is a slideshow. Only the emotional colour of
# each beat changes: the turn is a moment of warmth or recognition, never
# distress.
#
# Keyed by the same beats as `domain/duration.py`, which stays brand-agnostic.

    "context": "Where we are, who is near, and what this journey means to them.",
    "pressure": "Anticipation builds. The wait stretches, in a good way — eagerness, not anxiety.",
    "crack": "The turn: a small recognition or a moment of warmth that changes the feeling.",
    "acceleration": "Cuts shorten and the heart lifts. Momentum, never panic.",

#: What Veo *can* render that reads as IndiGo. Banning the logo without
#: supplying a replacement left the brand invisible: the guardrails said what
#: could not appear and nothing about what should.
INDIGO_BRAND_SURFACE = """
Because no mark and no type may be generated, brand presence has to be carried
entirely by things Veo can actually render. Every scene needs at least one:

- **The cabin.** Deep indigo navy-blue seat fabric, pale sky blue accents, clean
  white panels, generous bright daylight through the windows.
- **Crew.** Uniforms in deep indigo navy-blue, worn with calm, unhurried
  competence. Warm, professional, never obsequious or stiff.
- **The aircraft, abstracted.** Read the livery as blue-and-white *shapes* —
  partial fuselage, wing, tail curve, underside, shadow, reflection — never a
  clean broadside that would need readable lettering.
- **Blue in the world.** Boarding pass, bag tag, lanyard, seat-back, window
  reflection, tarmac light. Blue objects held, touched, and moved through.
- **The light.** Clean, bright, generous. IndiGo is a daytime airline; the frame
  should feel aired-out, never murky or moody.

A scene with no cabin, no crew, no aircraft form, and no blue object is not an
IndiGo scene. It is stock footage with a blue grade.
"""

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The video policy — the document this module exists for
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

INDIGO_VIDEO_BRAND_POLICY = """
========================
1. BRAND FOUNDATION IN MOTION
========================

IndiGo believes low-cost should never feel cheap. In film this is carried by how
the camera behaves, not by what is said.

Every generated film must feel:
- Effortless — nothing strains, nothing rushes
- On-time and precise — clean, deliberate framing
- Hassle-free — uncluttered frames, room to breathe
- Affordable without ever looking cheap

Brand personality on screen: fun, fresh, smart, simple. Confident, never arrogant.
Warm, never cheesy. Witty, never forced. Straightforward, never boring.

Core principle: say simple truths in interesting ways.


========================
2. THE MARK IS NEVER GENERATED  (ABSOLUTE)
========================

This is the single most-violated rule and the most damaging.

A generative video model cannot render a wordmark, a logotype, or a 20-dot logo
legibly. It will produce a misspelt, wrong-coloured, wrong-proportioned mark in
every clip. This also violates the brand book directly: the wordmark must come
from the master artwork and must never be typed.

Therefore, in every scene prompt:
- NEVER ask for the IndiGo logo, wordmark, logotype, or the arrow-plane device
- NEVER ask for the word "IndiGo" to appear anywhere in frame
- NEVER ask for the 20-dot pattern
- NEVER ask for goIndiGo.in or any URL on screen
- NEVER ask for aircraft tail lettering, fuselage lettering, livery text, or
  branded signage that would need readable brand type
- NEVER ask for subtitles, captions, supers, lower-thirds, end cards, or any
  on-screen text of any kind

All brand marks and all on-screen type are composited after the render, from the
master asset, in Bauhaus light, in sentence case. The generated footage is the
plate underneath, and nothing more.

When an aircraft appears, frame it so unreadable lettering is not the subject:
prefer partial fuselage, wing, underside, shadow, silhouette, or motion blur over
a clean broadside of a tail.

BUT: banning the mark does not mean banning the brand. See section 2b — the
brand still has to be visibly present in every scene, carried by things the model
can actually render.


========================
2b. HOW INDIGO IS MADE VISIBLE INSTEAD
========================
""" + INDIGO_BRAND_SURFACE + """


========================
3. COLOUR — AND WHY IT DRIFTS
========================

Palette:
- Dominant: deep indigo navy-blue with a violet lean (Pantone 2738 C)
- Secondary: pale sky blue (Pantone 291 C)
- Breathing space: clean white
- Any further colours must be soft and pastel

Blue must dominate the frame in EVERY scene, not on average across the film.

Strictly prohibited:
- Red-dominant palettes
- Purple-dominant palettes
- High-contrast or harsh palettes
- Sodium-orange or teal-and-orange grades

THE DRIFT PROBLEM. Each clip is a separate Veo generation with no memory of the
last one. A palette stated only in the treatment will be honoured in scene 1 and
gone by scene 6, and the film will change colour halfway through. The palette
therefore belongs in the continuity block AND restated inside every single scene
prompt, in colour words, never hex codes — a diffusion model responds to
"deep indigo navy-blue", not to a number.


========================
3b. EMOTIONAL REGISTER — WHAT THIS FILM IS FOR
========================

This is an advertisement. Its job is to make someone feel good about flying
IndiGo. Everything else serves that.

The craft references teach short-film dramaturgy, where tension is the engine.
Keep the *shape* — a film with no turn is a slideshow — but change the colour:

- The obstacle is never IndiGo, and never the journey. Distance, time apart, and
  missing someone are legitimate. Delays, queues, lost bags, cramped seats,
  turbulence, and airport stress are NOT.
- "Pressure" means anticipation, not anxiety. The wait to see someone you love
  is longing, not dread.
- The "crack" is a moment of warmth or recognition that turns the feeling — not
  a breakdown, a failure, or a loss of control.
- Nobody in an IndiGo film is frightened, panicked, distressed, tearful with
  grief, lost, or overwhelmed. Anticipation, longing, relief, delight, quiet
  contentment: yes. Fear and stress: never.
- Flying is the thing that RESOLVES the tension. The aircraft is where the story
  turns warm, never where it turns difficult.

If a beat seems to demand distress, express it as longing instead. A woman who
has not seen her mother in three years is full of anticipation, not fear.


========================
4. CAMERA AND MOTION
========================

"Effortless" is a camera behaviour first.

Preferred:
- Calm, precise, motivated moves — slow push-in, gentle lateral track, locked-off
- Smooth glides that echo the arrow-plane's forward/upward direction
- Generous negative space; let the frame breathe
- One primary move per shot, with a reason

Avoid:
- Frantic whip pans, snap zooms, aggressive speed ramps
- Shaky or chaotic handheld
- Restless drifting with no motivation
- Over-designed, busy, or cluttered frames

Direction of travel carries meaning: movement should read as forward or upward
wherever the story allows it.


========================
5. PEOPLE AND PHOTOGRAPHY
========================

Prohibited:
- Stock-photography look
- Posed or smiling stock faces
- Glamourised or cliché travel imagery
- Luxury-airline styling, and equally, discount-airline styling

Preferred:
- Candid, natural, unposed people
- Conceptual and insight-driven moments
- Evocative, atmospheric imagery
- Thoughtful or unexpected perspectives
- Destination-led storytelling

Aviation settings (tarmac, terminal, cabin, boarding) are permitted in a brand
film — but the aircraft is never the story. A hero beauty-shot of an aircraft, a
runway, or an airport with no human presence and no emotional beat is a cliché,
not a scene. Put a person and a feeling in the frame.


========================
6. SOUND AND ANY SPOKEN LINE
========================

- Room tone, texture, and the story's motif. Never ask Veo for music; a score is
  mixed in afterwards
- Any dialogue or voiceover follows the copy rules: sentence case, British English
  spellings (colour, favourite), Oxford comma, crisp and short
- Say "flights", never "routes" or "sectors". "Non-stop" is always hyphenated
- Never frame IndiGo as cheap or discount-heavy
- No shouty, promotional, or jargon-heavy delivery
- Smart, restrained wit. Effortless humour, never forced


========================
7. TYPOGRAPHY (POST ONLY)
========================

No type is ever generated into the footage. For the overlay stage:
- Bauhaus, light weight only, for titles and headlines
- Sentence case. Never ALL CAPS anywhere — titles, CTAs, or labels
- Clean, legible, minimal; never handwritten, serif, decorative, or script
- Never condensed, distorted, or heavily stylised


========================
8. ABSOLUTE NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS
========================

Never generate:
- The IndiGo logo, wordmark, arrow-plane, 20-dot device, or any on-screen text
- The word "IndiGo" rendered in frame in any form
- Cheap airline aesthetics or discount visuals
- Luxury airline clichés or over-premium styling
- Stock photography style outputs
- Red or purple colour palettes
- Cluttered or over-designed frames
- Parody or meme-style visuals
- Anything negative about IndiGo


========================
9. SAFETY AND CONTENT PROHIBITIONS (MANDATORY, ZERO TOLERANCE)
========================

Motion makes these far more dangerous than a still. Any scene that violates
these must be rewritten, not softened.

Aviation safety — strictly forbidden:
- Aircraft accidents, crashes, mid-air collisions, or emergency landings
- Aircraft in distress, on fire, smoking, or in uncontrolled descent
- Aircraft striking or colliding with any structure, terrain, or object
- Runway incidents, overruns, or taxiway collisions
- Severe turbulence, oxygen masks, emergency slides, or evacuations
- Any depiction of an aviation disaster, incident, or emergency scenario
- Crew or passengers behaving unsafely, or safety procedure shown incorrectly

Violence and destruction — strictly forbidden:
- Buildings, structures, or landmarks being struck, collapsing, or exploding
- Explosions, fires, or destruction in urban or public settings
- Mass harm, panic, or emergency evacuation
- Imagery evoking terrorism, sabotage, or deliberate attacks

General harmful content — strictly forbidden:
- Injury, death, or suffering of people or animals
- Conflict, warfare, or civil unrest
- Graphic or disturbing imagery of any nature
- Content that could distress, offend, or be read as harmful
- Anything that could damage the reputation of IndiGo or the aviation industry

These protect passenger safety perception and brand integrity. When in doubt,
choose the safer, more aspirational direction.
"""
